## Detailed rationales

### 1. **C — Output Evaluation and Validation**

A precise, uncited statistic is a classic fabricated-specific risk. The claim should be checked against an authoritative source or removed or explicitly labeled as unsupported. Softer wording does not create evidence, and a stronger model does not repair missing provenance.

### 2. **B — Prompting and Task Execution**

The revised prompt supplies role, audience and context, a clear task, material constraints, and the desired output format. The other options rely on vague quality language or invite unnecessary breadth without defining what a successful result looks like.

### 3. **C — Product and Model Selection**

The task recurs, its background is stable, and its output format is consistent, which strongly favors a Project. Research is unnecessary for a routine internal report, and repeatedly pasting context into Chat or Incognito preserves the setup tax.

### 4. **A and C — Workflow Integration and Solution Design**

Approving clause changes and signing or sending the contract carry non-transferable accountability and irreversible legal consequence. Clause extraction is reversible and mechanical, while a formula can be computed through code execution and then reviewed.

### 5. **B — Governance, Risk, and Responsible Use**

Processing authorization comes before persistence controls. Incognito can reduce ordinary history and Memory persistence, but it does not determine whether the document is allowed in that account, organization, or entry point.

### 6. **A — Troubleshooting and Optimization**

The failure appeared in the first response of a fresh chat, so missing context or constraints in the prompt is the cheapest and strongest initial hypothesis. Context overload and stale configuration require some form of accumulated or changed state.

### 7. **D — Configuration and Knowledge Management**

A reusable, ordered procedure belongs in a Skill. Memory is continuity, knowledge holds reference material, and a one-time prompt is not a maintainable shared procedure.

### 8. **A — Output Evaluation and Validation**

Accuracy and completeness fail independently. Correct sampled figures do not establish that every required factor was included, so the reviewer must compare the output against the full requirements and source set.

### 9. **B — Prompting and Task Execution**

Decomposition creates checkable intermediate results and keeps the recommendation tied to shared criteria. Recommending first or evaluating vendors in isolated conversations makes the reasoning inconsistent and less auditable.

### 10. **D — Workflow Integration and Solution Design**

The plan should rest on computed figures from the actual dataset, followed by synthesis using visible assumptions. Prose estimates, repeated guesses, or unrelated industry averages do not establish the organization’s real capacity needs.

### 11. **A — Product and Model Selection**

Haiku is the efficient starting point for high-volume, structured classification governed by a clear rubric, with exceptions reviewed by a person. Sonnet or Opus may be justified if measured quality fails, but the task does not initially require their higher reasoning ceiling.

### 12. **A, B, and C — Governance, Risk, and Responsible Use**

Skill trust depends on source and provenance, effective reach in the real session, and whether that reach is proportionate to the task. Interface preference does not establish safety, suitability, or authorization.

### 13. **C — Output Evaluation and Validation**

The table contains an internal contradiction, so the values must be recomputed and reconciled before use. Presentation quality and confident prose do not override arithmetic inconsistency.

### 14. **D — Configuration and Knowledge Management**

The current knowledge is already present; stale standing instructions and Memory are directing the workflow toward FY25. The repair is to update those maintained layers and run representative regression tests.

### 15. **B — Workflow Integration and Solution Design**

A personal helper has become infrastructure that multiple departments rely on for official reporting. That change in dependency triggers production concerns such as ownership, support, testing, security, monitoring, recovery, and architecture.

### 16. **C — Prompting and Task Execution**

Brainstorming benefits from range before filtering. Heavy constraints suppress divergence, so the workflow should generate broadly under essential guardrails and apply detailed requirements in a later selection pass.

### 17. **D — Product and Model Selection**

The symptom is long-session context degradation. A verified transfer summary and a clean conversation preserve necessary decisions without extending a degraded thread; recurring rules should also be persisted in the correct configuration layer.

### 18. **A and D — Output Evaluation and Validation**

Quote-first analysis exposes the evidence, while precise section or clause citations make claims auditable. Confidence language and unrestricted general knowledge make unsupported inference more likely, not less.

### 19. **A — Governance, Risk, and Responsible Use**

Final benefits determinations can materially affect people and require authorized, accountable human judgment. A stronger model or polished explanation does not transfer legal or organizational accountability to AI.

### 20. **A and C — Workflow Integration and Solution Design**

Credible communication states the bounded AI contribution and the retained human approval gate. “Fully automated” and hidden limitations overstate the workflow and create expectations the system cannot responsibly meet.

### 21. **B — Configuration and Knowledge Management**

The connector is operating within its defined boundary. A connector’s name does not imply universal read, draft, write, send, or delete capabilities, and a model upgrade cannot create an unavailable action.

### 22. **C — Troubleshooting and Optimization**

“Used to work” is the signature that points toward configuration drift. Instructions, sources, Skills, connectors, Memory, templates, and other maintained dependencies should be inspected before rewriting prompts or upgrading models.

### 23. **A — Prompting and Task Execution**

The output identifies the failing components: length, tone, and shape. Targeted changes preserve what already worked and make it possible to attribute the improvement to the corrected constraints.

### 24. **D — Output Evaluation and Validation**

The source is missing, the rule may have changed, and the stakes are regulatory. The draft can support expert review, but current authoritative material and a qualified human are required before reliance.

### 25. **B — Product and Model Selection**

This is a one-off drafting task with supplied context and no need for recurring configuration. Chat is the appropriate entry point, while an Artifact is useful when the email should be edited as a separate deliverable.

### 26. **C — Workflow Integration and Solution Design**

“Large number” allows materially different interpretations and architectures. A testable requirement must quantify concurrency, duration, response expectations, throughput, and acceptable failure conditions.

### 27. **B — Governance, Risk, and Responsible Use**

AI may assist with drafting, but the manager remains accountable for factual support, consistency, fairness, tone, and policy compliance. Disclosure depends on the organization and context, and employee correction or appeal rights must remain intact.

### 28. **C — Configuration and Knowledge Management**

An approved policy is authoritative reference material and belongs in Project knowledge. Selected evolving continuity may live in scoped Memory, but Memory should not replace the controlling source.

### 29. **A — Output Evaluation and Validation**

This is a capability hallucination. External action is established by tool results, system state, or logs, not by generated narration about what supposedly happened.

### 30. **D — Troubleshooting and Optimization**

A recurring correction is evidence about the system, not a permanent manual task. The requirement should be made observable, validated, and promoted into the narrowest correct layer, such as an instruction, Skill, query, code rule, or review gate.

### 31. **A, B, and D — Prompting and Task Execution**

The role frames the analysis, the context grounds it in the actual response set, and the countable task and output contract make the result actionable. “Insightful and polished” sounds useful but does not close a material specification gap.

### 32. **C — Product and Model Selection**

The task requires current, multi-source investigation with auditable citations, which points to Research. Sonnet is a defensible balanced tier for most professional synthesis, subject to actual product availability and evaluation results.

### 33. **A and C — Workflow Integration and Solution Design**

Receipt extraction and exact totals are reversible, mechanical steps suited to a document procedure and code execution. Approval and policy exceptions carry judgment and accountability that should remain human or collaborative.

### 34. **B — Governance, Risk, and Responsible Use**

The correct path begins with minimum necessary data and processing approval. Incognito affects ordinary persistence, not whether personal data may be processed, and partial redaction can leave powerful indirect identifiers.

### 35. **D — Output Evaluation and Validation**

Frequency is a computational claim, so counts should be produced by code execution over the actual responses. Representative quotes and approximate shares make the result both auditable and decision-ready.

### 36. **C — Configuration and Knowledge Management**

This instruction defines observable structure and explicit failure behavior for missing owners. The other options are aspirations that two readers could interpret differently and that cannot be tested reliably.

### 37. **A — Prompting and Task Execution**

Research prompts need a defined question, scope, currency requirement, source discipline, citations, and uncertainty handling. Unsourced breadth or a predetermined conclusion undermines the purpose of investigation.

### 38. **A, B, and C — Workflow Integration and Solution Design**

Reversibility, stakes, and accountability determine whether a workflow step should be AI-appropriate, collaborative, or human-retained. Model popularity has no bearing on responsibility allocation.

### 39. **B — Output Evaluation and Validation**

The prompt introduces confirmation bias by prescribing the desired conclusion. Neutral criteria, contrary evidence, and a controlled comparison help determine whether the original framing distorted the result.

### 40. **A — Troubleshooting and Optimization**

The prompt is already well specified, and the failure is a repeatable lack of depth on a speed-oriented tier. A controlled comparison that holds the task and evaluation constant tests whether model capability is the real cause.

### 41. **C — Product and Model Selection**

Exact multi-variable calculations should be executed rather than generated as prose. The formula, inputs, units, and edge cases still require review because code execution makes the result reproducible, not automatically correct.

### 42. **A and B — Governance, Risk, and Responsible Use**

The organization should contain the unapproved behavior and formally identify the Diligence gap. Durable remediation also removes the friction that made the noncompliant route easier; blame or blanket bans do not repair the system cause.

### 43. **D — Configuration and Knowledge Management**

Skills carry reusable procedures across relevant conversations, while Project instructions and knowledge remain scoped to the Project. Skills are not Project Memory and should not become repositories for Project-specific facts.

### 44. **C — Output Evaluation and Validation**

The improvement curve has flattened and the deliverable is high-stakes and external. Further prompting has diminishing returns, so the correct escalation is a qualified human review, not more unmeasured variation.

### 45. **B — Workflow Integration and Solution Design**

The measured bottleneck is formatting variance, so a shared formatting procedure removes the cause. A stronger model, earlier start, or more reviewers does not directly eliminate inconsistency.

### 46. **A — Prompting and Task Execution**

All three deliverables depend on the same factual interpretation of the policy. Extracting and verifying the common foundation first prevents one misreading from propagating across multiple drafts.

### 47. **D — Output Evaluation and Validation**

The reported figures must be computed from the actual spreadsheet, then delivered in a format suitable for reuse and review. An inline estimate or unrelated public total does not satisfy the reliability requirement.

### 48. **C — Governance, Risk, and Responsible Use**

Disclosure is contextual, but material uncertainty should not be resolved through concealment. The practitioner should check policy and obligations, seek authorized guidance, and use proportionate transparency where appropriate.

### 49. **A, B, and D — Output Evaluation and Validation**

Permission to say “not supported,” a bounded source set, and auditable citations reduce pressure to invent and make claims easier to verify. Confidence instructions do not create evidence.

### 50. **A — Configuration and Knowledge Management**

Duplicate, unlabeled policy versions create source-authority conflicts. The controlling version should be identified, superseded material removed or clearly archived, and provenance and review metadata maintained.

### 51. **A and B — Workflow Integration and Solution Design**

Audience adaptation may change vocabulary, order, examples, and depth. It must not change what the system actually does, who retains authority, the evidence supporting value claims, or material limitations and uncertainty.

### 52. **B — Troubleshooting and Optimization**

The session began correctly and degraded later, which makes context overload the primary hypothesis. A clean transfer summary fixes the current session, while a maintained instruction prevents the same recurring format rule from depending on old chat context.

### 53. **D — Prompting and Task Execution**

Analysis benefits from explicit criteria, standards, and scope; brainstorming benefits from latitude and range before filtering. The other options invert those task-type requirements.

### 54. **A — Output Evaluation and Validation**

Comparing candidates helps identify stronger framing before editing effort is invested. The final output still requires evaluation against requirements and human audience judgment; grammar or self-scoring alone is insufficient.

### 55. **C — Product and Model Selection**

Project conversations inherit Project instructions and knowledge, but they remain separate conversation threads. Shared Project configuration is not the same as automatic transcript-level context sharing across every chat.

### 56. **A and C — Governance, Risk, and Responsible Use**

Internal provenance lowers some uncertainty but does not establish current approval, policy fit, or least privilege. Effective reach depends on the session where the Skill runs, so your environment may expose more data and tools than the builder intended.

### 57. **D — Workflow Integration and Solution Design**

The response and account adjustment can affect money, customer rights, and organizational commitments. An authorized person must verify the facts and approve the side effect before the controlled system executes it.

### 58. **C — Troubleshooting and Optimization**

Without a baseline and target metric, the team cannot identify the real bottleneck or prove improvement. Instrumentation must precede optimization, and quality and governance metrics must remain guardrails.

### 59. **B and D — Output Evaluation and Validation**

Executed code can contain incorrect logic, definitions, filters, or edge-case handling, and the source data can be incomplete or misinterpreted. Reproducibility supports verification; it does not eliminate the need to validate code and inputs.

### 60. **A — Governance, Risk, and Responsible Use**

The scale, potential harm, contested fairness standard, and authority gap exceed ordinary individual judgment. A documented escalation is stronger than either an unsupported verdict or a technical substitution such as a stronger model.
