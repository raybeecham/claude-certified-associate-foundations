# Module 6 Quiz and Remediation Prompts

Use these prompts to review governance judgment after the Module 6 quiz. They are original study aids and should be adapted to current policy, law, product behavior, and organizational authority.

---

## 1. Explain each answer

```text
Review the five Module 6 quiz scenarios below.

For each scenario:
1. identify the primary governance concern;
2. name the controlling Module 6 concept;
3. explain why the correct answer is strongest;
4. explain why each distractor fails; and
5. state the retained human role or escalation path.

Do not rely on stronger-model reasoning when the real issue is delegation, data approval, permissions, policy, or fairness.
```

---

## 2. Use-case classification drill

```text
Classify this proposed AI use as:
- fully appropriate;
- appropriate with human review; or
- inappropriate for AI ownership.

Assess:
- reversibility;
- consequence of error;
- human creativity or empathy;
- accountability;
- load-bearing criterion;
- who / what / when review gate; and
- retained human authority.

Use case:
[describe a fictional use case]
```

---

## 3. Final-decision boundary

```text
A team wants AI to issue a final consequential determination.

Analyze:
- who is affected;
- what can go wrong;
- whether the decision is reversible;
- which authority must own the decision;
- whether AI may assist with preparation;
- what the human must independently verify; and
- what explanation, correction, or appeal path is required.

Conclude with:
- permitted AI role;
- prohibited AI role; and
- accountable human role.
```

---

## 4. Skill trust triage

```text
Evaluate this Skill before it is enabled.

Record:
- publisher and owner;
- current version and distribution path;
- instructions, scripts, dependencies, and bundled files;
- required runtime capabilities;
- files, connectors, tools, data, and external actions it could reach;
- data sensitivity;
- proportionality to the task;
- least-privilege changes;
- tests and monitoring;
- disable or rollback path; and
- Enable / Escalate / Decline decision.

Skill description:
[insert a fictional description]
```

## 5. Recommendation is not provenance

```text
A coworker recommends an executable AI Skill found online.

Explain why the recommendation does not establish:
- publisher trust;
- bundle integrity;
- safe dependencies;
- task-scoped permissions;
- approved data handling;
- organizational authorization; or
- acceptable external actions.

Then define the minimum evidence required before enabling it.
```

## 7. Data-processing versus persistence

```text
Evaluate a proposed confidential-data workflow in two separate stages.

Stage 1 — Processing authorization:
- data classification;
- approved account and organization;
- approved product and entry point;
- legal, contractual, and policy conditions;
- minimum necessary data; and
- authorized owner.

Stage 2 — Persistence controls:
- chat history;
- Memory;
- organizational retention;
- export;
- logging;
- sharing;
- deletion; and
- incident response.

Explain why a persistence control cannot authorize otherwise prohibited processing.
```

---

## 8. Incognito suitability test

```text
Determine whether Incognito is appropriate for this task.

First confirm:
- the data may be processed in the selected environment;
- the user is in the correct organization and account;
- the entry point is approved; and
- organizational retention and export are understood.

Then assess whether excluding the chat from ordinary history and Memory meets the persistence requirement.

Conclude with:
- Use Incognito;
- Use another approved path; or
- Stop and escalate.
```

---

## 9. Policy-to-practice audit

```text
Compare this team's observed AI use with the controlling policy.

For every gap, record:
- requirement and policy version;
- observed behavior;
- evidence;
- scope and frequency;
- affected parties and risk;
- root cause;
- immediate containment;
- durable corrective action;
- owner and due date;
- closure test; and
- re-audit date.

Do not treat every gap as individual misconduct. Inspect workflow friction, technical controls, ownership, staffing, incentives, and outdated configuration.
```

---

## 10. Friction-driven governance drift

```text
The approved AI route is slower or harder than an unapproved alternative.

Analyze:
- what policy requirement is being bypassed;
- what user need the workaround satisfies;
- what data or access is exposed;
- why reminders alone are unlikely to work;
- how to make the compliant path easier;
- which technical or workflow controls should change; and
- how closure will be verified.
```

## 11. Containment, remediation, closure

```text
Given this Diligence gap, separate the response into:

1. Immediate containment
2. Root-cause remediation
3. Verified closure
4. Re-audit trigger

Gap:
[describe the fictional gap]

Explicitly explain why containment is not the same as remediation and why a completion claim is not closure evidence.
```

## 12. Ethical concern localization

```text
Review this people-facing AI workflow.

Identify the most direct ethical concern among:
- bias or unfair treatment;
- transparency or disclosure;
- privacy;
- lack of human authority;
- lack of explanation or recourse;
- manipulation or power imbalance; and
- another clearly defined concern.

Explain why the selected concern is primary and why other concerns are secondary rather than irrelevant.
```

---

## 13. Hidden-exclusion review

```text
An AI workflow filters people out before an accountable human sees them.

Assess:
- who is excluded;
- selection criteria and proxy variables;
- source and representation bias;
- evidence standards;
- unequal error patterns;
- qualified review of exclusions;
- explanation and correction rights;
- monitoring for disparate effects; and
- escalation threshold.

Redesign the workflow so AI may assist without silently owning exclusion.
```

---

## 14. Fairness versus disclosure

```text
A workflow presents two ethical concerns: possible unfair treatment and unclear AI-use disclosure.

Determine which concern is most directly raised by the facts.

For each concern, state:
- supporting evidence;
- affected parties;
- severity and reversibility;
- required control; and
- whether it changes the use-case classification.

Do not collapse all ethical issues into one generic answer.
```

---

## 15. Accountability-transfer check

```text
Inspect this workflow for language that incorrectly transfers responsibility to AI.

Look for statements such as:
- the model decided;
- the system is responsible;
- Claude approved;
- the algorithm rejected; or
- no person owns the result.

Rewrite the accountability model to name:
- decision owner;
- reviewer;
- evidence;
- intervention authority;
- escalation; and
- recourse.
```

---

## 16. Stronger-model distractor test

```text
A proposed fix is to use a stronger model.

Determine whether the actual problem is:
- model capability;
- inappropriate delegation;
- missing human authority;
- unapproved data processing;
- excessive feature reach;
- policy drift;
- absent review gate;
- unfair workflow design; or
- another governance defect.

Explain why model capability does or does not address the root cause.
```

## 19. Full Module 6 synthesis

```text
Given a fictional AI-assisted workflow, perform an end-to-end Module 6 review.

Include:
1. use-case definition and affected parties;
2. Delegation criteria and load-bearing factor;
3. classification and human gate;
4. Skill or feature source, contents, and effective reach;
5. data classification and minimum necessary data;
6. approved environment and persistence controls;
7. controlling policy and observable controls;
8. Diligence gaps and corrective actions;
9. bias entry points and fairness standard;
10. disclosure, explanation, and recourse;
11. escalation conditions;
12. monitoring and re-review; and
13. final disposition: approve, constrain, redesign, defer, decline, or reject.
```

---

## Public-repository rule

Use fictional, generic, synthetic, public, or explicitly authorized scenarios. Do not include confidential policy text, internal audit findings, regulated records, private applicant or employee data, credentials, proprietary Skill bundles, incident details, remembered live-exam questions, or reconstructed proprietary course material.
