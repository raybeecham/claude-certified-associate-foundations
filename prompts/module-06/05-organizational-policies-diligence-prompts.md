# Module 6 Prompt Notebook: Organizational Policies and Diligence

Use fictional, synthetic, public, or explicitly authorized descriptions. Do not paste confidential policies, private audit records, restricted data, credentials, or identifiable personnel findings into public materials.

## 1. Policy-to-practice map

```text
Given this policy requirement:
[requirement]

Translate it into:
- observable practitioner behavior;
- workflow or technical control;
- required evidence;
- accountable owner;
- review cadence; and
- escalation condition.

Flag any wording that is too vague to audit.
```

## 2. Usage-audit scope

```text
Create a bounded AI-usage audit plan for:
[team, Project, or workflow]

Include:
- review period;
- policy version;
- approved products and entry points;
- data classes;
- Skills, connectors, and external actions;
- required human gates;
- exception records;
- authorized evidence sources;
- sample method;
- privacy limits on the audit itself; and
- accountable audit owner.
```

## 3. Diligence-gap register

```text
Compare this required practice:
[policy requirement]

with this observed practice:
[observation]

Create a Diligence-gap record containing:
- gap ID;
- policy requirement;
- observed practice;
- evidence;
- scope and frequency;
- risk and affected parties;
- root cause hypothesis;
- immediate containment;
- corrective action;
- owner and due date;
- closure test; and
- status.
```

## 4. Root-cause analysis

```text
A governance deviation occurred:
[description]

Analyze possible root causes across:
- policy clarity;
- discoverability;
- workflow design;
- technical access;
- staffing;
- incentives and deadline pressure;
- training;
- ownership;
- product or feature changes;
- outdated configuration; and
- exception management.

Do not default to individual blame.
```

## 5. Corrective-action hierarchy

```text
For this Diligence gap:
[gap]

Propose corrective actions in order of durability:
1. policy or ownership clarification;
2. workflow or configuration repair;
3. permission or technical control;
4. review and approval gate;
5. training or communication;
6. monitoring and re-audit.

Explain why reminders alone may be insufficient.
```

## 6. Review-gate bypass audit

```text
A required human-review gate was bypassed:
[scenario]

Assess:
- why the gate was bypassed;
- whether the reviewer was qualified and available;
- whether release was technically possible without approval;
- whether a backup reviewer existed;
- what immediate containment is needed;
- what workflow control should change;
- what evidence will prove closure; and
- how the next cycles should be monitored.
```

## 7. Approved-entry-point audit

```text
Review this proposed or observed use of an AI entry point:
[scenario]

Determine:
- data classification;
- controlling policy;
- approved or unapproved entry point;
- retention and persistence concerns;
- immediate containment;
- corrective configuration or communication;
- owner; and
- verification step.
```

## 8. Skill-vetting gap

```text
A Skill was enabled without a complete trust review:
[Skill description]

Create a remediation plan covering:
- source and ownership;
- bundle inspection;
- effective reach;
- data processed;
- least privilege;
- disable or restrict action;
- approving authority;
- testing;
- monitoring; and
- re-enable decision.
```

## 9. Policy freshness review

```text
Review this governance practice against a changing environment:
[current practice]
[known policy, product, or workflow changes]

Identify:
- assumptions that may be stale;
- policy version to confirm;
- affected configurations and Skills;
- affected users and data;
- required reapproval;
- regression tests; and
- next review date.
```

## 10. Exception record

```text
Draft a governance-exception record for:
[scenario]

Include:
- controlling requirement;
- reason the standard path cannot be followed;
- scope and duration;
- risk assessment;
- compensating controls;
- approving authority;
- owner;
- effective and expiration dates;
- monitoring;
- renewal criteria; and
- closure plan.
```

## 11. Exception-expiry audit

```text
Audit these existing exceptions:
[list]

For each, determine:
- whether it expired;
- whether scope expanded;
- whether compensating controls still operate;
- whether the approving authority remains valid;
- whether renewal is justified; and
- whether the exception should close, renew, narrow, or escalate.
```

## 12. Mini monthly audit

```text
Using this fictional month of AI usage:
[usage observations]

Produce:
- compliant practices;
- Diligence gaps;
- root causes;
- immediate containment;
- corrective actions;
- owners and due dates;
- closure tests;
- systemic themes; and
- next-month monitoring priorities.
```

## 13. Gap-closure verification

```text
A corrective action was reported complete:
[action and claimed result]

Design a closure verification that checks:
- whether observed behavior changed;
- whether the underlying control operates;
- whether users can still bypass it;
- whether new risks were introduced;
- whether evidence is retained; and
- whether re-audit is required.
```

## 14. Governance drift indicators

```text
Create measurable indicators for early governance drift in:
[workflow]

Consider:
- unapproved entry-point use;
- unvetted Skills;
- excessive connector permissions;
- skipped review gates;
- expired exceptions;
- stale policy references;
- missing owners;
- configuration changes without tests; and
- incidents or near misses.
```

## 15. Practitioner checklist

```text
Create a concise pre-use Diligence checklist for:
[role or workflow]

It should verify:
- use-case appropriateness;
- data classification;
- approved entry point;
- Skill and feature trust;
- human gate;
- policy version;
- exceptions;
- logging or evidence; and
- escalation route.
```

## 16. Oral certification drill

```text
Give me a fictional scenario involving a routine governance deviation.
Ask me to:
1. identify the controlling policy requirement;
2. identify the Diligence gap;
3. distinguish immediate containment from root-cause repair;
4. assign owner and closure evidence;
5. define re-audit timing.

Do not reveal the model answer until I respond.
```
