# Module 6 — Ethical Implications Prompt Notebook

These prompts support original study and practice. They are not a substitute for organizational policy, qualified review, or specialist ethics guidance.

## 1. Affected-party map

```text
Review this proposed AI-assisted use case:

[Describe the use case]

Identify:
1. direct users;
2. people whose opportunities, treatment, reputation, or access may change;
3. groups that may be overlooked;
4. decision-makers and accountable owners;
5. people with the least ability to challenge the result; and
6. possible beneficiaries and burdened groups.

Do not decide whether the use is ethical yet. Produce only the affected-party map and missing information.
```

## 2. Bias-entry-point audit

```text
Audit the following AI-assisted workflow for bias entry points:

[Workflow description]

Review separately:
- prompt framing;
- source material;
- labels and evaluation criteria;
- data or example selection;
- generated wording;
- omissions;
- human review; and
- downstream use.

For each possible bias source, state:
- the mechanism;
- who could be affected;
- what evidence would confirm or reduce the concern; and
- a practical control.
```

## 3. Fairness-standard definition

```text
For this use case:

[Use case]

Define what fair treatment should mean.

Address:
- which cases should be treated consistently;
- which differences are materially relevant;
- which characteristics or proxies should not influence the result;
- what evidence standard should be applied;
- how inconsistent outcomes would be detected; and
- what correction or appeal path is required.

Explain why identical treatment may or may not be sufficient.
```

## 4. Transparency and disclosure decision

```text
Evaluate whether AI assistance should be disclosed in this situation:

[Situation]

Assess:
- organizational policy;
- audience expectations;
- professional or contractual obligations;
- materiality of the AI contribution;
- risk of misleading authorship or expertise claims;
- potential consequence of concealment; and
- how human review changed the final work.

Conclude with:
- disclose;
- disclose with limited detail;
- no special disclosure required; or
- escalate for guidance.

Draft a proportionate disclosure only if supported by the analysis.
```

## 5. Six-part ethical review

```text
Conduct a structured ethical review of:

[Use case]

Use these sections:
1. Affected parties
2. Potential benefits
3. Potential harms
4. Fair-outcome standard
5. Transparency, human authority, and recourse
6. Decision, uncertainty, and escalation

End with one disposition:
Approve / Approve with constraints / Redesign / Defer / Reject.

State what evidence or authority is still missing.
```

## 6. Performance-review fairness check

```text
Review this fictional AI-assisted performance-summary workflow:

[Describe workflow or provide synthetic summaries]

Check for:
- unequal tone;
- unsupported personality or motivation claims;
- inconsistent evidence standards;
- different use of certainty;
- amplification of subjective notes;
- criteria not relevant to the role;
- missing employee context;
- disclosure and policy questions; and
- review or appeal weaknesses.

Design a pre-use human gate with Who / What / When / Authority / Escalation.
```

## 7. Pairwise consistency review

```text
Compare these two fictional evaluations:

[Evaluation A]
[Evaluation B]

Do not decide who performed better.

Identify whether:
- similar evidence is described with different tone;
- standards are applied inconsistently;
- unsupported traits are inferred;
- strengths and weaknesses receive unequal emphasis;
- uncertainty is expressed differently; and
- a reasonable reviewer would need more source evidence.

Return a consistency table and recommended corrections.
```

## 8. Ethical escalation test

```text
Determine whether this ethical question can be decided by the current team or requires escalation:

[Question and context]

Assess:
- number of people affected;
- severity and reversibility of harm;
- presence of vulnerable or protected groups;
- disputed fairness criteria;
- team expertise and authority;
- transparency obligations;
- recourse availability; and
- policy ownership.

Conclude:
Team may decide / Team may decide with constraints / Escalate.

If escalation is required, draft an issue brief containing facts, analysis, open questions, proposed controls, and the authority gap—without presenting a final verdict.
```

## 9. Disclosure-quality audit

```text
Evaluate this proposed AI-use disclosure:

[Disclosure]

Check whether it is:
- accurate;
- proportionate;
- understandable;
- noticeable;
- clear about human review;
- consistent with the actual workflow; and
- likely to mislead through omission.

Rewrite only if a repair is needed.
```

## 10. Policy-compliant but ethically weak

```text
Analyze this fictional use case that appears policy-compliant:

[Use case]

Identify ethical risks that formal compliance may not resolve, including:
- fairness;
- consent;
- transparency;
- power imbalance;
- surveillance;
- accessibility;
- recourse;
- labor effects;
- manipulation; and
- unequal distribution of benefit and harm.

Explain whether the use should remain approved, be constrained, be redesigned, be deferred, or be rejected.
```

## 11. Representative-case test plan

```text
Design an ethical test plan for this people-facing AI-assisted workflow:

[Workflow]

Include:
- representative user or subject groups;
- edge cases;
- consistent evidence and tone checks;
- proxy-characteristic checks;
- false-positive and false-negative consequences;
- reviewer qualifications;
- acceptable thresholds;
- escalation triggers;
- monitoring after deployment; and
- recourse for affected people.
```

## 12. Ethical decision record

```text
Convert this discussion into an ethical decision record:

[Notes]

Use:
- Use case
- Affected parties
- Potential benefits
- Potential harms
- Bias and fairness analysis
- Transparency decision
- Human authority and recourse
- Tests and evidence
- Residual uncertainty
- Escalation threshold
- Disposition
- Owner and review date

Do not invent missing evidence. Mark it explicitly.
```

## 13. Ethical reasoning critique

```text
Critique this ethical rationale:

[Rationale]

Check whether it:
- names affected people;
- describes concrete harms;
- defines a fair outcome;
- distinguishes policy compliance from ethical sufficiency;
- addresses disclosure;
- assigns human authority;
- provides recourse;
- acknowledges uncertainty;
- stays within the writer's authority; and
- defines monitoring or escalation.

Return strengths, gaps, and a repaired rationale.
```

## 14. Scenario generation

```text
Create five original fictional Module 6 scenarios about ethical implications of AI-assisted work.

Cover:
- biased framing;
- inconsistent evaluation;
- disclosure ambiguity;
- large-scale impact requiring escalation; and
- a policy-compliant but ethically questionable use.

For each scenario provide four plausible response options, the best answer, and a criterion-based explanation.

Do not reproduce proprietary course questions.
```

## 15. Oral certification drill

```text
Act as an oral examiner.

Give me one fictional ethical-implications scenario at a time.
Ask me to explain:
1. who is affected;
2. where bias could enter;
3. what fair treatment requires;
4. whether disclosure is needed;
5. what human review and recourse are required;
6. whether the team may decide or must escalate; and
7. the final disposition.

After my answer, grade the reasoning and identify one missed ethical dimension.
```
