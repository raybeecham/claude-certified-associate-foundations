# Module 6 Completion and Transition Prompts

These prompts reinforce the completed Governance, Risk & Responsible Use module and prepare the transition to troubleshooting.

Use fictional, generic, synthetic, public, or explicitly authorized scenarios.

---

## 1. Completion recall

```text
Summarize Module 6 in five durable principles.

For each principle, provide:
- the decision it governs;
- one common failure;
- one practical control; and
- one escalation trigger.
```

---

## 2. Full governance decision

```text
Evaluate this proposed AI-assisted use:

[describe the fictional use case]

Apply:
1. reversibility;
2. consequence of error;
3. human creativity or empathy;
4. accountability;
5. Skill or feature trust;
6. data classification and minimization;
7. current policy and approval;
8. bias, fairness, transparency, and recourse.

Identify the load-bearing criterion.
Conclude with Approve, Approve with constraints, Redesign, Defer, or Reject.
```

---

## 3. Human-gate check

```text
Review this proposed human-review gate:

[gate description]

Assess:
- who reviews;
- what they verify;
- when review occurs;
- evidence access;
- expertise;
- intervention authority;
- escalation; and
- retained approval evidence.

State whether the gate is operational or merely nominal.
```

---

## 4. Skill and feature trust recap

```text
Review this fictional Skill or integration:

[description]

Map:
- publisher and owner;
- version and distribution path;
- instructions, scripts, dependencies, and bundled files;
- session permissions;
- effective reach;
- data and external actions;
- least-privilege repairs;
- testing and monitoring; and
- disable or rollback path.

Choose Enable, Escalate, or Decline.
```

---

## 5. Data-control recap

```text
Classify this fictional dataset as Green, Yellow, or Red:

[data description]

Then determine:
- minimum necessary fields;
- direct and indirect identifiers;
- valid minimization or redaction;
- approved processing environment;
- history and Memory controls;
- retention and export considerations; and
- stop or escalation condition.
```

---

## 6. Policy-to-practice audit

```text
Compare this policy requirement with the observed workflow:

Policy:
[requirement]

Observed practice:
[behavior]

Create a Diligence-gap record with:
- controlling policy and version;
- evidence;
- scope and consequence;
- root cause;
- immediate containment;
- durable corrective action;
- owner and due date;
- closure test; and
- re-audit trigger.
```

---

## 7. Ethical-impact recap

```text
Review this people-facing AI-assisted workflow:

[workflow]

Identify:
- affected parties;
- bias entry points;
- possible harms and benefits;
- task-specific fairness standard;
- transparency or disclosure;
- human authority;
- explanation, correction, and recourse; and
- escalation threshold.
```

---

## 8. Governance versus troubleshooting

```text
A governed AI workflow is underperforming.

Explain which questions belong to governance and which belong to troubleshooting.

Governance questions should cover:
- appropriateness;
- data permission;
- feature trust;
- policy;
- fairness;
- accountability.

Troubleshooting questions should cover:
- observed failure;
- failure location;
- likely cause;
- smallest intervention;
- verification.
```

---

## 9. Guardrail preservation during repair

```text
A team proposes this performance fix:

[fix]

Check whether it weakens:
- data classification;
- permissions;
- human review;
- fairness checks;
- disclosure;
- logging;
- recourse; or
- accountability.

Redesign the fix so performance improves without bypassing governance.
```

---

## 10. Associate-path synthesis

```text
Explain how Modules 1 through 6 combine in one end-to-end AI workflow.

Map:
M1 — product and model selection
M2 — prompting
M3 — output evaluation
M4 — workflow integration
M5 — configuration
M6 — governance

Show where human judgment remains necessary in every module.
```

---

## 11. Oral certification drill

```text
Ask me ten short scenario questions covering Module 6.

Require me to identify:
- the governance concern;
- the controlling concept;
- the accountable role;
- the required control or gate; and
- the decision or escalation path.

After each response, grade the reasoning rather than keyword recall.
```

---

## 12. Transition readiness check

```text
Test whether I am ready to begin Module 7: Troubleshooting.

Give me five situations where an approved and governed workflow underperforms.
For each, ask me to separate:
- governance constraint;
- observed failure;
- suspected layer;
- safe diagnostic step; and
- evidence required before changing the system.
```

## Public-repository rule

Do not include confidential policies, regulated records, private employee or applicant data, proprietary Skill bundles, credentials, internal incidents, organization-only audit findings, remembered live-exam questions, or reconstructed proprietary course material.
