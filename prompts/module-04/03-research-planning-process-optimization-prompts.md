# Module 4 Notebook: Research, Planning, and Process Optimization

Use these prompts to separate research, executed analysis, synthesis, and human judgment in planning workflows.

---

## 1. Planning decision contract

```text
Define the planning decision before researching or calculating.

Planning question:
[QUESTION]

Identify:
- decision owner;
- intended action;
- decision deadline;
- stakeholders;
- required evidence;
- material calculations;
- assumptions;
- constraints;
- risk tolerance;
- approval authority; and
- success criteria.

Separate known facts, unknowns, assumptions, and human judgments.
```

---

## 2. Research-path selector

```text
Select the appropriate evidence path for each planning question.

Choose among:
- supplied source analysis;
- web search for straightforward current facts;
- deeper multi-source Research;
- approved internal systems;
- code-executed data analysis; or
- human clarification.

For each question, explain:
- why the path fits;
- expected source authority;
- currency requirement;
- verification method; and
- what remains unresolved.
```

---

## 3. Research evidence ledger

```text
Create a research evidence ledger.

For each material finding include:
- finding ID;
- claim;
- source;
- publication or effective date;
- exact support location;
- scope and population;
- source authority;
- conflicts;
- confidence;
- planning implication; and
- required validation.

Do not convert a research finding into a planning assumption without marking the transition.
```

---

## 4. Synthesis-versus-calculation split

```text
Analyze this planning task and separate every step into:
- research or retrieval;
- synthesis or interpretation;
- deterministic calculation;
- scenario generation;
- human judgment;
- approval; or
- external action.

For each step identify the appropriate owner and validation control.
```

---

## 5. Code-executed analysis contract

```text
Use code execution on the attached planning data.

Before computing:
1. inspect files and schema;
2. define date boundaries;
3. define inclusion and exclusion rules;
4. identify duplicates and missing values;
5. confirm units, currencies, and identifiers;
6. list unresolved business rules; and
7. mark provisional assumptions.

Then:
- execute the analysis;
- retain reproducible logic;
- show intermediate results;
- reconcile to available control totals;
- generate tables and charts from computed results; and
- state what still requires human judgment.
```

---

## 6. Capacity-planning analysis

```text
Using code execution on the attached workload and staffing data:
- calculate workload by quarter;
- calculate growth rates;
- calculate completed work per available staff member;
- analyze backlog and cycle-time trends;
- create baseline, conservative, and stress scenarios;
- estimate staffing needs under each scenario;
- show assumptions and formulas;
- create a sensitivity table; and
- identify decision factors not represented in the data.

Do not recommend hiring as an authorized action. Present a planning recommendation for the accountable owner.
```

---

## 7. Assumption register

```text
Create an assumption register for the plan.

For each assumption include:
- ID;
- statement;
- source or rationale;
- owner;
- status;
- affected calculation or scenario;
- sensitivity;
- validation method;
- review date; and
- action if disproven.
```

---

## 8. Scenario builder

```text
Build three planning scenarios from the verified evidence:
- baseline;
- conservative;
- stress.

For each scenario show:
- fixed inputs;
- changed assumptions;
- computed results;
- operational implications;
- risks;
- early warning indicators;
- reversible actions; and
- decisions requiring human approval.
```

---

## 9. Process bottleneck analysis

```text
Map the current planning workflow and identify the true bottlenecks.

For each stage capture:
- trigger;
- owner;
- input;
- work;
- output;
- wait time;
- rework;
- defect risk;
- decision or approval;
- system of record;
- exception path; and
- candidate improvement.

Distinguish information-retrieval, synthesis, calculation, handoff, review, state, and authority bottlenecks.
```

---

## 10. AI-leverage scan

```text
Review the workflow and identify where Claude adds meaningful value.

Classify each stage as:
- synthesis-heavy and AI-appropriate;
- collaborative;
- deterministic;
- human-retained;
- tool-owned; or
- storage-owned.

Explain why Claude should or should not participate at each stage.
```

---

## 11. Process-optimization proposal

```text
Propose a bounded workflow improvement.

Requirements:
- target the actual bottleneck;
- preserve source authority;
- route exact calculations to code;
- retain human judgment and approval;
- define state and handoffs;
- define failure and escalation paths;
- specify stage-level metrics; and
- use the least complex architecture that satisfies the need.
```

---

## 12. Planning recommendation audit

```text
Audit this planning recommendation.

Check:
- source traceability;
- source currency and scope;
- calculation reproducibility;
- business-rule correctness;
- assumptions and sensitivity;
- consistency between tables, charts, and narrative;
- hidden constraints;
- decision authority;
- limitations; and
- whether the recommendation overstates certainty.

Return Pass, Fail, or Needs Review for each dimension.
```

---

## 13. Human-retained decision brief

```text
Create a decision brief for the accountable owner.

Include:
- decision required;
- verified facts;
- computed evidence;
- assumptions;
- scenarios;
- trade-offs;
- constraints not represented in the data;
- risks;
- reversible next steps;
- required approvals; and
- explicit questions for human judgment.
```
