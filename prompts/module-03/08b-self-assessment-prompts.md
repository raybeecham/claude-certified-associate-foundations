# Module 3 Notebook: Self-Assessment — Score Your Own Conversation

Use these prompts to review your own AI-assisted behavior without copying confidential or identifying conversation content into the repository.

This self-assessment is optional and qualitative. It is not a scored certification result.

---

## 1. Conversation selection prompt

```text
Help me choose one recent AI conversation to review.

Select a conversation that:
- produced something I considered using, sharing, or acting on;
- involved enough substance to evaluate;
- can be discussed without exposing confidential information; and
- illustrates a real decision about trust, verification, or audience fit.

Do not request or reproduce sensitive conversation details. Return only a generic description of the conversation type and why it is suitable for reflection.
```

---

## 2. Task reconstruction

```text
Reconstruct the intended task from this sanitized conversation summary.

Summary:
[GENERIC DESCRIPTION]

Identify:
- objective;
- audience;
- decision or action supported;
- evidence available;
- expected output;
- consequence if wrong; and
- likely success criteria.

Mark anything not supported by the summary as `unknown`.
```

---

## 3. Discernment review

```text
Assess my Discernment behavior in this conversation.

Evaluate whether I checked:
- fit to the actual question;
- requirement coverage;
- accuracy;
- completeness;
- source support;
- internal consistency;
- bias or framing;
- audience fit;
- output format; and
- the correct next action.

Return:
1. evidence of strong Discernment;
2. missed or weak checks;
3. one highest-value improvement; and
4. a revised review checklist for the next similar conversation.
```

---

## 4. Diligence review

```text
Assess my Diligence behavior in this conversation.

Evaluate whether I:
- checked the output before using it;
- matched review depth to the stakes;
- verified material claims;
- recomputed material calculations;
- confirmed any external action;
- applied required human review;
- checked sensitive-data handling;
- preserved uncertainty; and
- retained accountability for release.

Return:
1. evidence of strong Diligence;
2. missing controls;
3. whether the output was safe for the stated use; and
4. one concrete pre-release control for next time.
```

---

## 5. Two-sentence reflection builder

```text
Turn this self-assessment into exactly two sentences.

Sentence 1 must begin with `Strong:` and name one observable behavior that worked.
Sentence 2 must begin with `Tighten:` and name one specific control I will use next time.

Avoid vague phrases such as `be more careful`, `verify everything`, or `write better prompts`.
```

---

## 6. Generic-prompt progression review

```text
Analyze this learning progression:

Earlier behavior:
My prompts were generic and did not define enough context, constraints, evidence, or output requirements.

Current behavior:
I now think about how to request the output and understand that I must verify it before relying on it.

Map the progression to:
- task specification;
- Discernment;
- Diligence;
- grounding;
- deterministic verification;
- human-review thresholds; and
- release decisions.

Then identify the next maturity step beyond the general intention to verify.
```

---

## 7. Verification-specificity converter

```text
Convert the statement `I will verify the output` into an operational verification plan.

For the use case:
[USE CASE]

Define:
- material claims;
- authoritative source for each claim;
- calculations requiring code;
- consistency and completeness checks;
- stakes and review threshold;
- required reviewer;
- release blockers; and
- evidence to retain.
```

---

## 8. Discernment indicator checklist

```text
Score the conversation against these Discernment indicators using Yes, Partial, No, or Not Applicable:

- The response was compared with the real question.
- Every requested element was checked.
- Accuracy and completeness were reviewed separately.
- Material claims were checked against evidence.
- Contradictions and unsupported certainty were examined.
- Bias and framing were considered.
- Audience and format fit were reviewed.
- The correct next action was identified.

For every Partial or No, recommend one small workflow change.
```

---

## 9. Diligence indicator checklist

```text
Score the conversation against these Diligence indicators using Yes, Partial, No, or Not Applicable:

- The output was checked before use.
- Review depth matched the consequence of error.
- Material factual claims were verified.
- Material calculations were recomputed.
- External actions were confirmed.
- Sensitive-data handling was authorized.
- Required human review occurred before release.
- Uncertainty and limitations were preserved.
- Accountability remained with the releasing person or organization.

For every Partial or No, identify the missing control and its correct placement in the workflow.
```

---

## 10. Reflection maturity assessment

```text
Classify this reflection on the following maturity ladder:

0. Accepts output because it sounds useful.
1. Notices obvious errors after use.
2. Reviews requirements and visible defects before use.
3. Verifies material claims and calculations systematically.
4. Predefines evidence, tests, review gates, and release criteria.
5. Measures recurring failure patterns and improves the workflow.

Reflection:
[REFLECTION]

Return:
- current level;
- supporting evidence;
- one reason not to assign the next level yet; and
- the behavior required to reach the next level.
```

---

## 11. Prompt-versus-output balance

```text
Review whether I focused too much on prompt quality and not enough on output evaluation.

Compare:
- how much effort went into specifying the task;
- how much effort went into checking the result;
- whether success criteria existed;
- whether evidence and calculations were tested;
- whether the intended use was considered; and
- whether a release decision was explicit.

Recommend a balanced workflow for the next conversation.
```

---

## 12. Stakes calibration reflection

```text
Evaluate whether my review effort was proportionate.

Use case:
[DESCRIPTION]

Assess:
- consequence if wrong;
- reversibility;
- audience;
- regulatory, contractual, or policy exposure;
- evidence quality;
- uncertainty; and
- whether the output triggered action.

State whether I under-reviewed, over-reviewed, or reviewed proportionately, with the correct review level for next time.
```

---

## 13. Conversation postmortem

```text
Conduct a short postmortem on a conversation whose output required correction.

Use only sanitized facts.

Identify:
- original objective;
- prompt gap;
- output defect;
- missed Discernment check;
- missed Diligence control;
- point where the defect should have been caught;
- consequence or near miss;
- corrected workflow; and
- one preventive habit.
```

---

## 14. Strength-to-standard converter

```text
I believe I was strong at:
[STRENGTH]

Convert that strength into a repeatable standard by defining:
- trigger;
- required behavior;
- evidence of completion;
- failure condition; and
- example use.
```

## 15. Tightening-action designer

```text
My improvement area is:
[AREA]

Turn it into one concrete behavior that is:
- observable;
- tied to a workflow stage;
- proportionate to risk;
- easy to repeat; and
- verifiable after completion.

Return a one-sentence commitment and a checklist.
```

## 16. Weekly reflection log

```text
Create a weekly AI-assistance reflection log with these fields:

- conversation or task type;
- intended use;
- stakes;
- strongest prompting behavior;
- strongest Discernment behavior;
- strongest Diligence behavior;
- defect or near miss;
- verification performed;
- human review used;
- release disposition;
- one next-week habit; and
- evidence that the habit was applied.

Do not include confidential content or full conversation transcripts.
```
