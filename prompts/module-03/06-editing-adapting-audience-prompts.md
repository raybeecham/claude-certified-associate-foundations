# Module 3 Notebook: Editing and Adapting for Audience

These prompts help transform verified drafts into audience-specific deliverables. They do not replace fact-checking, disclosure review, organizational approval, or qualified professional judgment.

Use fictional, generic, synthetic, public, or explicitly authorized information.

---

## 1. Audience-contract builder

```text
Define the audience contract before rewriting this material.

Identify:
- audience and role;
- subject-matter expertise;
- purpose of the communication;
- decision, action, or response expected;
- attention available;
- channel and format;
- tone and relationship;
- disclosure restrictions;
- material facts, uncertainty, risks, conditions, and obligations that must remain visible.

Return a compact audience-contract table. Do not draft the final communication yet.
```

---

## 2. Verified-content model

```text
Create a verified-content model from the supplied analysis.

Separate:
1. factual claims;
2. figures, units, dates, and denominators;
3. source-supported conclusions;
4. inference and hypotheses;
5. uncertainty and confidence;
6. material risks and dependencies;
7. legal, contractual, policy, or professional obligations;
8. approved organizational commitments; and
9. internal-only or restricted information.

Label each item as:
- invariant across audiences;
- adaptable in presentation; or
- restricted from the target audience.

Do not add facts not present in the supplied material.
```

---

## 3. Three-pass editor

```text
Edit this verified draft in three labeled passes.

Pass 1 — Clarity:
- remove repetition;
- tighten loose sentences;
- clarify vague references;
- lead with the main point;
- preserve facts, conditions, and uncertainty.

Pass 2 — Tone:
- adapt the register for [AUDIENCE AND RELATIONSHIP];
- do not increase certainty or conceal risk.

Pass 3 — Formatting:
- structure the result for [CHANNEL AND USE];
- make the decision, action, or next step easy to find.

After the three passes, provide an invariant-check table showing that all required facts, caveats, and obligations remain.
```

---

## 4. Executive-summary adapter

```text
Adapt the verified analysis for an executive audience.

Lead with:
1. decision or conclusion;
2. measurable impact;
3. material risk or uncertainty;
4. requested action or approval.

Keep the main body concise. Move supporting method detail to a labeled appendix rather than deleting it.

Preserve:
- material figures and units;
- conditions that affect the conclusion;
- uncertainty;
- decision-changing risks;
- dependencies; and
- required obligations.

Return:
- executive summary;
- decision request;
- appendix notes; and
- invariant-check table.
```

---

## 5. Working-team adapter

```text
Adapt the verified analysis for the working team.

Preserve:
- evidence and method;
- assumptions;
- dependencies;
- owners;
- dates;
- open questions;
- risks;
- unresolved uncertainty; and
- next actions.

Use operational headings:
- Current state
- Evidence
- Drivers and constraints
- Decisions needed
- Actions, owners, and dates
- Open questions

Distinguish facts, hypotheses, and unresolved items explicitly.
```

---

## 6. Client-facing adapter

```text
Adapt the verified internal analysis into a client-facing communication.

Requirements:
- preserve supported facts, figures, uncertainty, and commitments;
- remove internal-only speculation, debate, and restricted information;
- avoid unsupported causal claims;
- use approved terminology and professional tone;
- identify agreed actions, owners, and update dates only when supported;
- retain caveats that affect client interpretation; and
- flag any wording that requires contractual, legal, communications, or accountable-owner review.

Return:
1. client-facing draft;
2. removed or restricted internal content log;
3. commitment and disclosure check; and
4. required approval list.
```

---

## 7. Regulator-or-auditor adapter

```text
Adapt the verified content for a regulator, auditor, or formal oversight reviewer.

Prioritize:
- applicable requirement;
- evidence and source location;
- methodology;
- exception or gap;
- control or corrective action;
- owner and status;
- uncertainty and limitation;
- approval and version information.

Remove promotional language and unsupported persuasion.

Return a traceable structure that allows the reviewer to verify each material statement.
```

---

## 8. Public-audience adapter

```text
Adapt the verified content for a general public audience.

Use accessible language while preserving:
- essential facts;
- material uncertainty;
- safety or action information;
- approved public commitments; and
- required disclosures.

Remove:
- confidential information;
- internal jargon;
- unapproved speculation;
- technical detail that is unnecessary for understanding.

Do not simplify so aggressively that the message becomes misleading.
```

---

## 9. Selective-omission audit

```text
Compare the full verified analysis with the shortened audience version.

For every omitted item, classify it as:
- safe to omit for this use;
- moved to supporting detail;
- decision-critical and must be restored;
- legally, contractually, or professionally required;
- restricted and correctly removed; or
- unclear and requiring human review.

Identify whether any omission changes the meaning, confidence, risk, or expected action.
```

---

## 10. Tone-without-distortion review

```text
Review the adapted draft for tone changes that may distort the evidence.

Flag:
- increased certainty;
- softened material risk;
- hidden disagreement;
- advocacy presented as neutral analysis;
- diplomatic language that obscures an obligation;
- urgency not supported by the source;
- overconfident promises or commitments; and
- emotional or persuasive framing that exceeds the evidence.

For each issue, provide a truth-preserving revision.
```

---

## 11. Candidate-draft comparison

```text
Compare these candidate drafts against one common rubric.

Criteria:
1. factual fidelity;
2. source and caveat preservation;
3. completeness for the intended audience;
4. clarity;
5. tone;
6. structure and scanability;
7. actionability;
8. disclosure control;
9. compatibility with approved commitments; and
10. editing effort required.

Do not select by polish alone.

Return:
- scored comparison;
- strongest base draft;
- strengths worth preserving from other candidates;
- incompatible assumptions or claims that must not be merged; and
- validation required after combination.
```

---

## 12. Multi-model comparison boundary

```text
Compare drafts produced by different models or runs.

First verify that all drafts use the same:
- source package;
- requirements;
- audience contract;
- evidence boundary;
- definitions; and
- success criteria.

Then compare presentation quality.

Do not treat agreement as factual proof or disagreement as evidence that the majority is correct. Identify all claims requiring authoritative verification.
```

---

## 13. Invariant-check table

```text
Compare the adapted output with the verified-content model.

Create a table with:
- invariant item;
- source wording or value;
- location in adapted output;
- preserved exactly / preserved with equivalent meaning / missing / distorted;
- required correction.

Check facts, figures, units, dates, conditions, uncertainty, risks, dependencies, and obligations.
```

---

## 14. Disclosure and recipient review

```text
Review this audience-adapted output for disclosure risk.

Assess:
- recipient authorization;
- confidential or internal-only material;
- personal or sensitive information;
- proprietary methods or debate;
- unapproved commitments;
- legal privilege or protected material;
- security-sensitive detail;
- information required to remain visible; and
- downstream forwarding or publication risk.

Return:
- safe content;
- content to remove or generalize;
- content requiring approval;
- missing required disclosure; and
- release recommendation.
```

---

## 15. Raw-to-executive transformation drill

```text
Transform the supplied raw analysis into an executive version.

Constraints:
- maximum 120 words;
- lead with impact and decision;
- include no more than three key facts;
- preserve any condition or uncertainty that could change the decision;
- include a clear requested action;
- do not invent ownership, timing, or causation.

Then explain what was removed, moved, and retained.
```

---

## 16. Raw-to-working-team transformation drill

```text
Transform the supplied raw analysis into a working-team update.

Include:
- current state;
- evidence and method;
- drivers and dependencies;
- risks and uncertainty;
- action items;
- proposed owners and dates labeled as proposed when not approved;
- open questions; and
- next review point.

Do not convert hypotheses into facts.
```

---

## 17. Cross-audience consistency review

```text
Compare the executive, working-team, client, and public versions of the same analysis.

Identify:
- fact or figure mismatches;
- inconsistent certainty;
- risks visible in one version but misleadingly absent in another;
- incompatible commitments;
- disclosure violations;
- audience-appropriate differences; and
- wording that could cause different groups to reach conflicting interpretations.

Return a reconciliation plan and corrected common content model.
```

---

## 18. Final-deliverable review

```text
Review the finished audience-adapted deliverable before release.

Verify:
- factual fidelity;
- completeness for purpose;
- audience fit;
- clarity;
- tone;
- format;
- uncertainty and risk preservation;
- disclosure control;
- required reviewer approval;
- version consistency; and
- correct release channel and recipient.

Return one disposition:
- Release;
- Edit and re-review;
- Verify;
- Escalate; or
- Reject.
```

---

## 19. Oral certification drill

Answer each in two or three sentences:

1. Why is an accurate draft not necessarily finished?
2. What are the three editing passes?
3. What is an audience contract?
4. What must remain invariant across versions?
5. What may change across versions?
6. What is misleading omission?
7. Why compare candidates before editing?
8. Why is the most polished candidate not automatically best?
9. What should an executive version lead with?
10. What must be reviewed before an external version is released?
