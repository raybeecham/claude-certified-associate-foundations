# What Module 6 established

Module 6 converted responsible use from a general intention into a structured practitioner workflow.

```text
Define the use case and affected parties
      ↓
Screen with Delegation criteria
      ↓
Evaluate Skill and feature trust
      ↓
Classify and minimize data
      ↓
Apply current organizational policy
      ↓
Audit real practice and close Diligence gaps
      ↓
Review bias, fairness, transparency, and recourse
      ↓
Approve, constrain, redesign, defer, decline, or reject
```

The module's central principle is:

> Technical capability does not establish appropriateness, permission, fairness, or accountable authority.

# Capabilities now developed

## 1. Use-case classification

The learner can assess:

- reversibility;
- consequence of error;
- need for human creativity, empathy, or relationship ownership; and
- accountability.

The learner can identify the load-bearing criterion and classify a use as:

```text
Fully appropriate
Appropriate with human review
Inappropriate for AI ownership
```

A human-review classification includes an operational **who / what / when** gate before use or consequence.

## 2. Skill and feature trust

The learner can treat a Skill as software and review:

- publisher and owner;
- version and distribution path;
- instructions, scripts, dependencies, and bundled files;
- effective reach across the actual session;
- data and external actions;
- least privilege;
- testing and monitoring; and
- disable or rollback paths.

```text
Internal
      ≠
Vetted
```

The learner can choose **Enable**, **Escalate**, or **Decline** under the correct authority.

## 3. Data sensitivity and feature controls

The learner can classify data as Green, Yellow, or Red; determine the minimum necessary data; and distinguish direct identifiers from re-identification risk.

```text
Permission to process
      ≠
History behavior
      ≠
Memory behavior
      ≠
Retention or export
```

The learner understands that Incognito, Memory controls, sandboxing, retention, and deletion are bounded controls rather than processing authorization.

## 4. Organizational policy and Diligence

The learner can translate policy into observable behavior, controls, evidence, ownership, and escalation.

```text
Written policy
      ↓
Observed practice
      ↓
Diligence gap
      ↓
Containment
      ↓
Root-cause repair
      ↓
Verified closure and re-audit
```

The learner can bound audits, manage exceptions, assign corrective actions, and distinguish containment from remediation and verified closure.

## 5. Ethical implications

The learner can identify affected parties, inspect bias entry points, define a task-specific fairness standard, determine disclosure expectations, and preserve explanation, correction, and recourse.

```text
Accurate wording
      ≠
Fair treatment
      ≠
Ethical sufficiency
```

The learner can escalate when scale, possible harm, vulnerable groups, contested fairness standards, unclear disclosure duties, or organizational standing exceed the team's authority.

# Five durable takeaways

```text
1. Governance is a practitioner skill.
2. Screen use cases with Delegation criteria.
3. Treat a Skill as software.
4. Classify data before it enters a feature.
5. Review ordinary outputs for bias, fairness, and transparency.
```

These takeaways operate together rather than independently.

```text
Appropriate use
+ trusted feature
+ approved data path
+ current policy
+ ethical review
+ accountable human authority
=
responsible operating decision
```

# Transition to Module 7

Module 6 asked:

```text
Should this use proceed,
under which boundaries,
and who remains accountable?
```

Module 7 asks:

```text
Why is the approved workflow underperforming,
where is the failure located,
and what is the smallest responsible repair?
```

The transition is:

```text
Governed use case
      ↓
Observed underperformance
      ↓
Failure classification and localization
      ↓
Targeted intervention
      ↓
Verification and optimization
```

Governance remains active during troubleshooting. A performance fix must not bypass data, permission, fairness, review, or accountability controls.

# Short recap

```text
1. Decide whether the use is appropriate before optimizing it.
2. Preserve accountable human ownership for consequential outcomes.
3. Treat executable capabilities as software with effective reach.
4. Classify and minimize data before it enters a feature.
5. Apply current policy during routine work, not only formal reviews.
6. Audit real behavior and verify that corrective actions worked.
7. Review bias, fairness, disclosure, explanation, and recourse.
8. Escalate questions beyond your expertise or authority.
9. Record the rationale, controls, approvals, and review date.
10. Monitor outcomes because responsible use is a lifecycle.
```

> Claude remains useful at scale when the surrounding organization makes responsible judgment routine rather than exceptional.
