# Overview

Self-assessment turns the module from a set of ideas into a review of actual behavior.

The exercise asks two simple questions about a recent AI-assisted conversation:

1. **Discernment:** Did I judge whether the response actually fit the question and requirements?
2. **Diligence:** Did I check the output before using it at a level appropriate to the stakes?

> Responsible AI use is not demonstrated by knowing the vocabulary. It is demonstrated by changing what you do before relying on an output.

---

# Plain-English explanation

Think about one recent conversation and ask:

```text
Did I ask clearly enough for the result I needed?
          ↓
Did I inspect whether the answer actually met that need?
          ↓
Did I verify the important parts before using them?
```

The first two questions emphasize **Discernment**. The third emphasizes **Diligence**.

A useful reflection names both:

- where the workflow was already strong; and
- one specific behavior to tighten next time.

The goal is not to criticize every past conversation. The goal is to identify one repeatable improvement.

---

# One analogy: reviewing game film

An athlete does not improve only by learning the rules. They review footage of what they actually did.

The film review asks:

- What decision did I make?
- What information did I notice?
- What did I miss?
- What should I repeat?
- What should I change next time?

Conversation self-assessment works the same way.

The prompt and output are the game film. Discernment reviews whether the response fit the play that was called. Diligence reviews whether the result was checked before it affected the score.

> Reflection is useful when it produces a concrete next behavior, not merely a general intention to be more careful.

# The two behavioral indicators

## Discernment indicator

Ask:

> Did I judge whether the response actually fit the question I asked, rather than merely whether it read well?

Evidence of Discernment includes:

- checking every requested element;
- comparing the output with the supplied sources;
- separating accuracy from completeness;
- identifying unsupported claims or contradictions;
- checking audience and format fit;
- noticing when the answer solved a different problem; and
- assigning an explicit verdict or next action.

Weak pattern:

```text
The answer sounded professional, so I used it.
```

Stronger pattern:

```text
I compared the answer with the requirements, found that one requested risk was missing, and revised it before use.
```

## Diligence indicator

Ask:

> Did I check the output before using it at a level appropriate to the model, task, and stakes I was relying on?

Evidence of Diligence includes:

- validating material claims against sources;
- recomputing important calculations;
- checking tool or external-action results;
- applying mandatory human-review gates;
- confirming sensitive-data handling;
- preserving uncertainty;
- stopping iteration when expertise or authority was required; and
- retaining accountability for the released result.

Weak pattern:

```text
Claude produced the total, so I copied it into the report.
```

Stronger pattern:

```text
I reran the total through code, reconciled it to the source file, and obtained the required reviewer approval.
```

# Interpreting the learner reflection

The reflection identifies an important progression.

## Earlier behavior

```text
Generic prompt
      ↓
Broad output
      ↓
Limited evaluation discipline
```

Generic prompts can produce responses that are difficult to judge because the success criteria, evidence boundary, audience, format, and uncertainty behavior are not explicit.

## Current learning

```text
Define the task clearly
      ↓
Specify context, evidence, constraints, and output
      ↓
Evaluate fit and completeness
      ↓
Verify material claims and calculations
      ↓
Apply review proportional to the stakes
```

The reflection therefore demonstrates:

- awareness of prompt specification;
- awareness that output quality must be judged against the intended task; and
- awareness that verification is required before reliance.

## Strong point

A clear strength is the shift from treating prompting as a casual request toward treating it as a specification-and-evaluation workflow.

## Tightening opportunity

The next improvement is to make verification concrete rather than general.

Instead of only saying `verify everything`, define:

- which claims are material;
- which source or test verifies each one;
- which calculations require code;
- what review depth the stakes require; and
- what conditions block release.

```text
General intention: Verify the output.

Operational control:
For each material claim, identify the authoritative source or test,
record the result, and escalate any unresolved high-stakes issue.
```

# A practical self-assessment protocol

```text
1. Select one recent conversation
          ↓
2. Restate the real task and intended use
          ↓
3. Review the prompt specification
          ↓
4. Review the output against requirements and evidence
          ↓
5. Review the verification and release behavior
          ↓
6. Name one strength
          ↓
7. Name one specific improvement
          ↓
8. Define the next-conversation habit
```

## Step 1: Select a conversation

Choose one that produced something you considered using, sharing, or acting on.

Do not place confidential or identifying conversation content in the public repository.

## Step 2: Restate the task

Record:

- objective;
- intended audience;
- intended decision or action;
- evidence available;
- output format; and
- consequence if wrong.

## Step 3: Score Discernment behavior

Check whether you evaluated:

- requirement fit;
- accuracy;
- completeness;
- source support;
- consistency;
- bias;
- audience fit; and
- format.

## Step 4: Score Diligence behavior

Check whether you:

- verified important claims;
- used code for material calculations;
- confirmed external actions;
- applied human-review thresholds;
- checked sensitive-data handling;
- preserved uncertainty; and
- documented the release decision.

## Step 5: Write two sentences

Use:

```text
Strong: I was effective at [SPECIFIC BEHAVIOR], which helped [RESULT].

Tighten: Next time I will [SPECIFIC CONTROL] before [USE OR RELEASE POINT].
```

# Example reflection

```text
Strong: I clearly defined the intended audience and required output, then checked that every requested section was present.

Tighten: Next time I will create a claim-evidence table and recompute the material figures before copying the result into a client-facing draft.
```

This is stronger than:

```text
I did pretty well, but I should be more careful.
```

The stronger reflection names an observable behavior and a future control.

# Indicator checklist

## Diligence

- [ ] I checked the output before using it.
- [ ] The depth of checking matched the stakes.
- [ ] Material claims were verified against appropriate evidence.
- [ ] Material calculations were recomputed or tested.
- [ ] Required human review occurred before release.
- [ ] External actions were confirmed through tools or system state.
- [ ] I retained accountability for the result.

## Discernment

- [ ] I compared the response with the actual question and requirements.
- [ ] I checked accuracy and completeness separately.
- [ ] I looked beyond fluent wording and confident tone.
- [ ] I checked source support and contradictions.
- [ ] I considered bias, audience fit, and format.
- [ ] I identified the correct next action rather than accepting the first draft.

# Reflection maturity ladder

| Level | Behavior |
|---:|---|
| 0 | Accepts the answer because it sounds useful |
| 1 | Notices obvious mistakes after use |
| 2 | Reviews requirements and visible defects before use |
| 3 | Verifies material claims and calculations systematically |
| 4 | Predefines evidence, tests, review gates, and release criteria |
| 5 | Measures recurring failure patterns and improves the workflow |

The purpose is not to assign a permanent label. It is to identify the next control that would improve the workflow.

---

# Common anti-patterns

## Reflection limited to prompt quality

**Failure:** The learner improves the prompt but does not inspect the output.

**Repair:** Review both task specification and observed result.

## General promise to verify everything

**Failure:** No source, test, or reviewer is identified.

**Repair:** Define claim-level or risk-based verification methods.

## Treating every output as high stakes

**Failure:** Routine work is over-reviewed.

**Repair:** Match Diligence to consequence and intended use.

## Treating low stakes as no review

**Failure:** Even obvious requirement or factual defects are ignored.

**Repair:** Use proportionate basic review.

## Scoring only the model

**Failure:** The human workflow, prompt, evidence, tools, and release decision are excluded.

**Repair:** Assess the complete system of use.

## Reflection without a next behavior

**Failure:** The exercise does not change future practice.

**Repair:** End with one observable habit and trigger.

# Knowledge check

## Question 1

What does the Discernment indicator ask?

**Answer:** Whether the user judged that the response actually fit the question, requirements, evidence, and intended use—not merely whether it read well.

## Question 2

What does the Diligence indicator ask?

**Answer:** Whether the user checked the output before relying on it at a depth appropriate to the task, model behavior, and stakes.

## Question 3

Why is `I will verify everything` incomplete?

**Answer:** It does not identify which claims matter, what sources or tests apply, who must review them, or what blocks release.

## Question 4

What is the strongest part of the recorded reflection?

**Answer:** It recognizes the shift from generic prompting toward deliberate specification and verification.

## Question 5

What is the next improvement suggested by the reflection?

**Answer:** Convert the general verification intention into explicit claim-level, calculation, and review controls.

# Short recap

```text
1. Review one real conversation, not an abstract example.
2. Ask whether the response actually fit the task.
3. Ask whether important content was checked before use.
4. Separate Discernment from Diligence.
5. Name one specific strength.
6. Name one specific tightening action.
7. Match verification depth to the stakes.
8. Turn the reflection into a repeatable next-conversation habit.
```

> The value of self-assessment is not the score. It is the next behavior you change.
